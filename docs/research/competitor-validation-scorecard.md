## Finite trial protocol

After the user selects the employer workflow, compare at least EloCard, Withe and the employer's existing ATS/form. Include HiHello if the employer already owns it, and Yello if it is a realistic procurement option. A competitor is not disqualified merely because it serves larger companies or requests a quote.

Use a fictional company and synthetic candidate data in a test account. Do not contact actual candidates. For each relevant product:

1. Record the exact plan, region, date, billing basis and available features.
2. Configure one event, two roles, two staff members and a versioned privacy notice.
3. Capture two synthetic candidates and retry one submission to check duplication.
4. Assign the required next action, change its owner and inspect overdue/unresolved reporting.
5. Change the notice and inspect whether earlier submissions preserve their original evidence.
6. Queue a follow-up to a controlled test inbox, withdraw the candidate's permission before dispatch, and observe the result. Distinguish a preference change from a deletion request.
7. Export and import into the actual target workflow. Confirm which fields and evidence survive.
8. Open the application link. Confirm the system does not count a completed application without supporting evidence. If an ATS integration exists, submit a synthetic application in its test environment and inspect reconciliation.
9. Close the event and inspect retained records, pending sends and private-link access against the configured policy.
10. Record success, failure, unavailable-in-plan, or not-tested, with redacted evidence. Do not convert not-tested into failure.

No such accounts have been created, emails sent, or subscriptions purchased in this research pass. Avoid mixing prospective trial steps with completed evidence.

## Employer evidence template

For each interview, record only information the employer permits us to retain:

- Employer identifier, buyer role, next event and existing tools.
- Exact recent workflow failure, frequency and consequence.
- Existing workaround and staff time spent, with the source of any estimate.
- Why an already available competitor or ATS feature fails to resolve it.
- The single acceptance scenario that would justify switching or purchasing.
- Budget owner, proposed pilot terms and actual response; distinguish interest from commitment.

An opinion that “this sounds useful” is not evidence of an unmet need or willingness to pay.

## Decision rule and stop point

Do not build the proposed workspace solely because the combined workflow looks attractive. Continue only after the user agrees the direction and accessible employers show a repeated problem that existing options fail to solve at acceptable total cost. A service/setup offering is a valid alternative if the tools already work and implementation is the actual problem.

The decisive remaining evidence is buyer-specific workflow and hands-on behavior. More broad competitor lists cannot establish it. Without a selected direction or customer/tool evidence, stop extending speculative feature lists and preserve the research checkpoint.
